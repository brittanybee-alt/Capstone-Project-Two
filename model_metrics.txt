CAPSTONE TWO - FINAL MODEL METRICS
Project: Where You Grow Up Matters - Predicting Adult Income for Low-Income Children in the DC Region
==============================================================================

FINAL MODEL
  Model type ........... Linear regression (ordinary least squares, no penalty)
  Library .............. scikit-learn LinearRegression (default settings, fit_intercept=True)
  Target ............... kfr_pooled_pooled_p25 (mean household income rank, 0-1, of children
                         raised at the 25th percentile of parent income; measured 2014-2015)
  Training data ........ 588 census tracts (80% split, random_state=42, stratified by state)
  Testing data ......... 147 census tracts (20%, used once at the end)
  Hyperparameters ...... none (plain linear regression has no tuning settings)
  Why this model ....... Best cross-validated score was the random forest, but linear regression was
                         within one standard error of it and is simpler and easier to explain.

FINAL MODEL PERFORMANCE
                                   Cross-validation (training)    Test set (held out)
  R-squared .................              0.745  (+/- 0.017 SE)          0.833
  MAE (rank units) ..........             0.0308                     0.0284
  RMSE (rank units) .........             0.0420                     0.0363
  MAE in plain words: predictions miss by about 2.8 percentile points of income rank on average (test set).

MODEL SELECTION RULE
  One-standard-error rule: pick the simplest model whose cross-validated R-squared is within one
  standard error of the best model. Best = Random forest (0.757, SE 0.014), cutoff = 0.743; linear,
  ridge, lasso and the forest were all at or above the cutoff, so the simplest (linear) was chosen.
  Cross-validation: 5-fold on the training set; ridge, lasso and the forest tuned inside each fold.

ADDITIONAL CHECKS
  Test R2 without the state dummies .......... 0.824 (with them: 0.833)
  Average test error by state (percentile pts)  DC 2.7, MD 2.4, VA 3.2
  Average test error, lower vs upper half of predictions: 2.5 vs 3.2 percentile points
  Bottom-quartile screening: 89% of the test tracts in the actual bottom quartile were also in the model's bottom quartile

LIMITATIONS
  Small sample (735 tracts); estimates are noisier for tracts with few children; outcomes are for children
  born 1978-1983 while predictors describe 2000-2015; results are associations, not cause and effect.
